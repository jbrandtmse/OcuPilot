# Prose review: OcuPilot PRD addendum

Reviewed: `addendum.md` (6,347 words by `word_metrics.py`). Reader type: humans. Style guide: Microsoft Writing Style Guide. Copy-edit only: no fact, number, date, row ID, class name, path or decision is changed by any row below.

## Purpose and audience read

This document exists to help the architect, the UX designer, the story writer and the builder pick up the depth the PRD left out, and to give any later reader the audit trail of the 22 decisions the owner made while the PRD was written.

## Relationship to the structure pass

The structure findings in `polish-structure-addendum.md` were read first. Rows that fall inside a CONDENSE'd passage (decisions 16, 17, 18, 19, 21 and 22; section 10's note; section 14) review the passage as it stands and say so in the Changes column, so the condensed rewrite can absorb the fix. The one MERGE'd sentence (the REST-path resolution) and the MOVE'd bullets (token lifetime, unobserved direction, the section 3 directive, section 14) are reviewed at their surviving location; nothing in them needs a prose fix beyond what is listed.

## Style, tone and voice (preserve)

- **A compressed reference register.** Bold lead-in fragments closed with a period; colon-led lists ("Rejected:", "Candidate:", "Observed exceptions:"); semicolon-packed inventories; row IDs, FR numbers and spike rows cited in parentheses; "X is Release 1", "is step 7", "is polish-week" as the idiom for where a thing lands. This is the document's scanning grammar for four readers who dip in by section. Every row below works inside it; none unpacks a fragment into a sentence or a semicolon list into prose.
- **Clinical and owner-attributed.** "The owner kept", "the reviewer costs", "the catalog rates": the actor is always named, hedging is absent, and directives to downstream readers are plain imperatives ("Build them into", "reference in place, never copy"). No em-dashes or en-dashes occur anywhere in the file, so the house rule needs no enforcement.
- **American English with two strays.** "license", "normalized", "authorization", "favorites" throughout; "synchronisation" and "licence" are the only British forms. The serial comma is omitted in about twenty lists and present in two.

Observed, not a prose fix (the figures belong to the owner): decision 22 says "about 45 developer-days against 19" while the section 15 total row says "46 to 47". Left as written.
