# Structure review: OcuPilot PRD addendum

Reviewed: `addendum.md` (6,347 words by `word_metrics.py`; section 1 is 1,586 of them). Reader type: humans. Style guide: Microsoft Writing Style Guide. No length target.

## Purpose and audience read

This document exists to help the architect, the UX designer, the story writer and the builder pick up the depth the PRD deliberately left out (auth transport, API backing, harvest sources, install mechanics, portal parity obligations, sizing) and to give any later reader the audit trail of the 22 decisions the owner made while the PRD was written.

Standing constraints honored throughout: content is sacrosanct; every numbered decision in section 1 survives (condensed where marked); every row of the protected tables (auth transport, web application settings, API backing, harvest map, risk register, contest facts, sizing, feasibility budget) is untouched. Every word-impact figure below comes from the `word_metrics.py` output or from per-line counts taken over the same file.

## Structure model

Reference/Database for sections 2 to 15; Strategic/Context (Pyramid) for section 1.

**Against the Reference model (sections 2 to 15).** Random access mostly holds: fifteen numbered sections, table-led, each self-contained, and every protected table intact. Two misses. First, there is no reader routing: four named readers, fifteen sections, and no line telling the architect that sections 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 are theirs or the UX designer that 7 and 8 are. Second, MECE leaks between section 1 and the fact sections: a decision and its downstream fact are both stated in full for the REST path (decision 19 and section 6), the alerts.log endpoint (decision 16 and section 3), CP-28 (decision 15 and section 10) and Open Question 15 (decision 18, section 2 and section 15). Schema is consistent inside the tables; the "(open for architecture)" marker that section 2 uses is missing from the two open items in section 6.

**Against the Pyramid (section 1).** The list is chronological, not top-down. The decisions that reshape everything downstream (21, safety invariants; 22, build order as cut line) sit at the bottom, and the decisions they amend (2, 5) read as current because nothing in them points forward. Chronology is the right order for an audit record, so the fix is pointers and a short lead, not resequencing.

The risk register's overlap with sections 2, 6 and 7 is not a MECE failure: the register is the consolidated view and the sections are the working detail. It is marked PRESERVE below.

## Closing summary

- **Recommendations:** 23 (1 CUT, 2 MERGE, 4 MOVE, 8 CONDENSE, 4 QUESTION, 5 PRESERVE; several rows carry two tags).
- **Estimated reduction if all are accepted:** with §9 preserved, about −277 words net (reductions −377, scaffolding +100), 4.4% of 6,347. With §9 cut to a pointer, about −418 words, 6.6%. Section 1 falls from 1,586 to about 1,320 words (−17%) while keeping all 22 decisions; the protected tables (roughly 2,400 words across §2, §3, §5, §9, §10, §12, §13, §15) are untouched.
- **Length target:** none provided. The document is a reference companion; its length is governed by the protected tables, so a large cut was never available and none is proposed.
- **Comprehension trade-offs:** the three big condenses (decisions 16 to 19, 21, 22) make the audit record depend on the PRD and the reconcile and review files for sentence-level tracing; someone auditing from the addendum alone loses "which sentence changed" granularity. Cutting §9 would remove the only copy of the competitive table outside the research folder. The four additions (+100 words: routing line, forward pointers, section-1 lead, open-for-architecture markers) are human-reader scaffolding and the strongest comprehension gains in the set.
